# SAR narrative structure

FinCEN's SAR Narrative Guidance Package (November 2003) sets out how a narrative should
be organised. The agent's case-note template mirrors this structure.

> When all applicable information is gathered, analyzed, and documented and the financial
> institution decides that a SAR is required, the information should be described in the
> SAR Narrative in a concise and chronological format. Include all elements of the five
> W's (Who? What? When? Where? and Why?) ... We suggest that you divide the narrative
> into three sections: an introduction, a body, and a conclusion.

**Introduction** — the purpose of the SAR and a general description of the known or
alleged violation. FinCEN's own examples of what to name at the outset: "Informal Value
Transfer System (IVTS) operations, smurfing, shell entities, complex layering activities,
structuring, check kiting, embezzlement." Also: any previously filed SARs on the same
suspect, any OFAC connection, internal reference numbers, and "a summary of the 'red
flags' and suspicious patterns of activity that initiated the SAR."

**Body** — the supporting facts:

- all relevant facts about the parties who facilitated the activity, including "suspected
  shell entities; financial activities which are not commensurate with the expected normal
  business flows and types of transactions; unusual multiple party relationships; ...
  unusual and/or complex series of transactions indicative of layering; lack of business
  justification";
- "a specific description of the involved accounts and transactions, identifying if
  known, both the origination and application of funds (usually identified in
  chronological order by date and amount)";
- transactor and beneficiary information, including the name and location of any involved
  financial institutions, account numbers, dates and amounts;
- "an explanation of any observed relationships among the transactors."

**Conclusion** — any additional information the institution can make available to law
enforcement.

FFIEC Appendix L adds the sixth question explicitly:

> **How did the suspicious activity occur?** The narrative should describe the "modus
> operandi" or the method of operation of the subject conducting the suspicious activity.
> For example, if what appears to be structuring of currency deposits is matched with
> outgoing funds transfers from the accounts, the SAR narrative should include information
> about both the structuring and outbound transfers (including dates, destinations,
> amounts, accounts, frequency, and beneficiaries of the funds transfers).

FinCEN also notes what NOT to do: do not write "see attached" or rely on supporting
documents, because only explicit narrative text is captured into the SAR database.
